Motivation: Why Long-Horizon VLA Is Brittle

  • Flat end-to-end VLA often imitates trajectories instead of solving stages.
  • In long horizons, small errors accumulate → later stages collapse.
  • No explicit “what subgoal am I doing now?” signal.
Hypothesis: Conditioning the policy on an explicit subtask can reduce trajectory overfitting and improve stability.
Speaker: Chuxi (0:55). Emphasize accumulation + lack of explicit stage signal.

Packaged Story: A Controlled Study with an Oracle High-Level Planner

Goal: isolate the effect of hierarchy on long-horizon execution.

  • Use oracle / rule-based subtask decomposition.
  • Compare against flat end-to-end prompting.
  • Test: Does hierarchy help? When does it fail?

Two factors we analyze:

  • A Explicit subtask prompts (split rubric)
  • B Subtask-specific finetuning of π0 on (task, subtask)

We’re not claiming a perfect planner—just exposing the potential of hierarchical VLA.

Speaker: Chuxi (0:55). Make clear: oracle planner for analysis; two-factor framing.

Two-Level Hierarchical VLA (Overview)

  • Low level: π0 executes each subtask with stable, reusable primitives.

Rubrics (inference prompting)

  • Baseline rubric use RoboTwin full prompt end-to-end.
  • Split rubric replace with current subtask prompt each stage.

Prompt (baseline):

First, place red block on the far left, then green block in the middle, and finish with blue block on the right.

Prompt (split):

Models (training)

  • pi0-base original π0 (no subtask finetuning).
  • pi0-split π0 finetuned on data with (task, subtask) prompts.
Key idea: single shared model learns many subtasks (not one model per subtask).

Training & Evaluation Protocol (Important Implementation Detail)

π0 training

  • Imitation learning with sliding window (sequence chunks).
  • Policy predicts a chunk of action tokens each step.

RoboTwin evaluation

  • Loop: predict 50 steps (~1s) → simulator executes → next observation.
  • This is synchronous; differs from async real-world
Speaker: Ji (1:15). Stress sync chunked control (50 steps ~1s) and why it affects stability.

Discussion: What We Learn from the Oracle Planner

  • Hierarchy helps when subtasks are reusable: Task 1 shows reduced trajectory coupling and better robustness.
  • Alignment matters: finetuning without matching inference prompting can hurt (Task 1, pi0-split under baseline rubric).
  • Some tasks need recovery: Task 2 suggests decomposition alone is insufficient without corrective behaviors.
